Sentence Rhythm: Using Flow Score to Fix Monotone Writing

Two drafts can contain identical ideas and feel completely different to read. Often the difference is rhythm.

What rhythm means

Rhythm comes from the mix of long and short sentences. Five sentences of about fifteen words each read like a metronome. Mix a short one with a longer one that unspools an idea, then a medium one, and the reader stays alert.

How Flow Score reads your text

Flow Score sorts each sentence into one of six length bands: 1–5, 6–10, 11–15, 16–25, 26–35 and 36 or more words. Each band has its own colour in the highlighted view. The score rises when your sentences spread across several bands and falls when you stack several of similar length in a row. Those runs are underlined with a wavy line.

Fixing a flat passage

  • Find an underlined run and split its longest sentence in two.
  • Join two short, related sentences with a comma and conjunction, or a dash.
  • Put a very short sentence after a long one when you want emphasis.

A caution

A high score doesn’t mean good writing. Legal text, instructions and technical writing may rightly use steady, even sentences. Treat the score as a prompt to look again, and trust your ear over the number.
