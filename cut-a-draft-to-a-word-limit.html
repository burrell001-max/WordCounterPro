How to Cut a Draft to a Word Limit

Being 200 words over a limit feels worse than it is. Most drafts carry far more than 200 words of filler.

Start with the numbers

Paste the draft into the counter and note how many words you need to lose. Turn that into a per-paragraph target. Trimming ten words from each of twenty paragraphs is easier than cutting one paragraph you love.

Cut in this order

  1. Throat-clearing openings. Phrases like “It is important to note that” add nothing. Delete them and begin with the point.
  2. Doubled words. “Clear and obvious”, “plans and goals”. Keep the stronger word.
  3. Weak intensifiers. Words like “very”, “really” and “quite” rarely help.
  4. Repeated points. If two sentences say the same thing, keep the better one.
  5. Long lead-ins. “In the event that” becomes “if”. “Due to the fact that” becomes “because”.

Use the tools

Keyword density shows words you overuse, and find & replace lets you hunt down a habit such as “that” or “just” and decide, one by one, whether each is needed. Set a maximum goal so the progress bar tells you the moment you’re inside the limit.

Know when to stop

Don’t trim until the writing feels breathless. If you’re still over, cut a whole example or section rather than squeezing every sentence. Read the result aloud with Proof read to check it still sounds natural.
